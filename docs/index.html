Can machine learning read a chase better than history?

After every ball of an IPL chase we estimate how likely the chasing team is to win, from the scoreboard alone, and test whether machine-learning models beat a simple table of historical win rates.

Graders: the write-up covers the question, data, preprocessing, models, evaluation, results, error analysis and limitations. The results page has every table, and the repository lets you reproduce it all. Below, replay any test-season chase ball by ball.

Reading the chart

Each line is the chasing team's chance of winning, recalculated after every ball from the scoreboard alone: runs needed, balls left, wickets left, the target and the runs and wickets in the most recent few legal balls. Once the result is settled by the rules (target reached, all out, or no balls left) the lines jump to 100% or 0% and the ball is marked decided.

Ball-by-ball data from Cricsheet (ODC-By). ES 335 project by Vinit Parmar and Dutt Parmar. Code, data splits and full results: GitHub repository.
